The Book of Prayer - Travellers

64 flows · 100% of hadith grouped · hadith Nos. 1570–1950

#TopicHadithCount
  1. 261

    Prayer was prescribed as two rak'ahs and kept so on journeys; shortening is Allah's charity, and the Prophet, Abu Bakr, Umar and Uthman prayed only two rak'ahs travelling.

    1570–1580The Book of Prayer - Travellers11
  2. 262

    The Prophet shortened to two rak'ahs at Dhu'l-Hulaifa and after three miles or farsakhs, and kept praying two rak'ahs during a ten-day stay in Mecca until returning to Medina.

    1581–1589The Book of Prayer - Travellers9
  3. 263

    The Prophet, Abu Bakr and Umar prayed two rak'ahs at Mina, as did Uthman at first before praying four, which Ibn Mas'ud regretted; Ibn Umar prayed four behind the imam.

    1590–1599The Book of Prayer - Travellers10
  4. 264

    On cold, windy or rainy nights the Prophet had the caller tell people to pray in their dwellings, as Ibn Umar and Ibn Abbas did, even on a rainy Friday.

    1600–1609The Book of Prayer - Travellers10
  5. 265

    On journeys the Prophet prayed voluntary prayers and witr on his mount facing wherever it went, but not obligatory ones; the verse on Allah's face wherever you turn concerns this.

    1610–1620The Book of Prayer - Travellers11
  6. 266

    When hurrying on a journey the Prophet combined sunset with Isha and noon with afternoon, delaying the first prayer to the time of the second.

    1621–1627The Book of Prayer - Travellers7
  7. 267

    The Prophet combined noon with afternoon and sunset with Isha in Medina without fear, rain or journey, and on the Tabuk expedition, so his Ummah would not face hardship.

    1628–1637The Book of Prayer - Travellers10
  8. 268

    After prayer the Prophet turned to the left as well as to the right, and companions liked to stand at his right so his face would turn to them.

    1638–1643The Book of Prayer - Travellers6
  9. 269

    Once the iqama is given only the obligatory prayer is prayed; the Prophet questioned men who prayed alone after the dawn prayer had begun.

    1644–1651The Book of Prayer - Travellers8
  10. 270

    Entering the mosque one asks Allah to open the doors of His mercy and prays two rak'ahs before sitting, as the Prophet told Jabir and did after journeys.

    1652–1659The Book of Prayer - Travellers8

    Aisha said the Prophet prayed the forenoon prayer only after journeys, or prayed four rak'ahs or more; Umm Hani saw him pray eight rak'ahs when Mecca was conquered.

    1660–1670The Book of Prayer - Travellers11
  12. 272

    1671–1675The Book of Prayer - Travellers5
  13. 273

    The Prophet prayed two short rak'ahs between the call and iqama of the dawn prayer, kept them more than any voluntary prayer, and said they are better than the world.

    1676–1689The Book of Prayer - Travellers14
  14. 274

    In the two rak'ahs before the dawn prayer the Prophet recited 'Say: O unbelievers' and 'Say: Allah is One', or verses from al-Baqara and Al Imran.

    1690–1693The Book of Prayer - Travellers4
  15. 275

    Allah builds a house in Paradise for whoever prays twelve voluntary rak'ahs a day, and Ibn Umar listed those he prayed with the Prophet before and after the obligatory prayers.

    1694–1698The Book of Prayer - Travellers5
  16. 276

    Aisha described the Prophet's long night prayer standing or sitting, bowing from the position he recited in, and in old age reciting seated but standing to recite before bowing.

    1699–1707The Book of Prayer - Travellers9
  17. 277

    In his last year or two the Prophet prayed most voluntary prayers sitting; he said a seated prayer is worth half, but he is not like others.

    1708–1716The Book of Prayer - Travellers9
  18. 278

    Aisha said the Prophet prayed eleven or thirteen rak'ahs at night with witr, in Ramadan or not, then two short rak'ahs before dawn, and that his heart did not sleep.

    1717–1727The Book of Prayer - Travellers11
  19. 279

    The Prophet slept early and rose late in the night or at cockcrow to pray, loved regular deeds, and after the two dawn rak'ahs talked with Aisha or lay down.

    1728–1733The Book of Prayer - Travellers6
  20. 280

    The Prophet prayed witr every night, early, at midnight or late, finishing it by dawn, and woke Aisha to pray her witr.

    1734–1738The Book of Prayer - Travellers5
  21. 281

    Aisha told Sa'd b. Hisham the Prophet's character was the Quran, night prayer became voluntary after a year, and described his witr of nine, later seven, rak'ahs.

    1739–1742The Book of Prayer - Travellers4
  22. 282

    When illness or sleep kept the Prophet from night prayer he prayed twelve rak'ahs by day, and a night portion of Quran missed is recorded if recited before noon.

    1743–1745The Book of Prayer - Travellers3

    The Prophet said the prayer of the penitent is observed when young weaned camels feel the heat of the sun.

    1746–1747The Book of Prayer - Travellers2
  24. 284

    Night prayer is offered in pairs of rak'ahs, and one fearing dawn adds a single rak'ah; witr is one rak'ah at the end of the night prayer, prayed before morning.

    1748–1759The Book of Prayer - Travellers12
  25. 285

    Night prayer is in pairs closed by one rak'ah; observe witr before morning, early if one may not wake, otherwise at the end of the night, which is witnessed.

    1760–1767The Book of Prayer - Travellers8
  26. 286

    The most excellent prayer is the one in which the standing is longest.

    1768–1769The Book of Prayer - Travellers2
  27. 287

    Every night there is an hour in which any Muslim who asks Allah for good in this world and the next is given it.

    1770–1771The Book of Prayer - Travellers2
  28. 288

    Allah descends every night to the lowest heaven, asking who will call on Him, ask of Him or seek His forgiveness, until daybreak.

    1772–1778The Book of Prayer - Travellers7
  29. 289

    Praying at night in Ramadan or on Lailat-ul-Qadr with faith and hope of reward brings forgiveness of past sins; the Prophet stopped leading Ramadan night prayer lest it become obligatory.

    1779–1784The Book of Prayer - Travellers6
  30. 290

    Ubayy b. Ka'b swore that Lailat-ul-Qadr is in Ramadan, on the twenty-seventh night, and that the sun rises next morning bright without rays.

    1785–1787The Book of Prayer - Travellers3
  31. 291

    Spending a night at Maimuna's, Ibn Abbas joined the Prophet's night prayer, was moved from his left to his right, and heard him pray for light within and around him.

    1788–1797The Book of Prayer - Travellers10
  32. 292

    At night the Prophet recited the last verses of Al Imran, prayed thirteen rak'ahs with witr, and moved Ibn Abbas from his left to his right.

    1798–1804The Book of Prayer - Travellers7
  33. 293

    On a journey the Prophet prayed in one garment tied at opposite ends and moved Jabir, standing behind him, to his right.

    1805The Book of Prayer - Travellers1
  34. 294

    The Prophet began his night prayer with two short rak'ahs and told others to do the same.

    1806–1807The Book of Prayer - Travellers2
  35. 295

    Rising to pray at night the Prophet praised Allah as Light and Lord of the heavens and earth and asked guidance and forgiveness, with set words for bowing and prostrating.

    1808–1813The Book of Prayer - Travellers6

    At night the Prophet recited al-Baqara, al-Nisa and Al Imran before bowing, pausing to glorify, ask and seek refuge, and prayed so long Abdullah thought of sitting.

    1814–1816The Book of Prayer - Travellers3
  37. 297

    The devil urinates in the ear of an all-night sleeper and ties three knots on sleepers, undone by remembrance, ablution and prayer; the Prophet urged Ali and Fatimah to pray.

    1817–1819The Book of Prayer - Travellers3
  38. 298

    Pray some prayers at home and do not make houses graves; a man's prayer at home is best except the obligatory, and Satan flees a house where al-Baqara is recited.

    1820–1826The Book of Prayer - Travellers7
  39. 299

    Allah loves best deeds done constantly, even if small; the Prophet had Zainab's prayer rope untied and, about a woman praying all night, said to do what one can sustain.

    1827–1834The Book of Prayer - Travellers8

    1837–1844The Book of Prayer - Travellers8

    1845–1852The Book of Prayer - Travellers8
