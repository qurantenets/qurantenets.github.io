  1. 385

    No Zakat is due below five wasqs of dates or grain, five camels or five uqiyas of silver; river- or rain-watered crops owe a tenth, camel-watered a twentieth.

  2. 386

    No Zakat is due from a Muslim on his slave or horse, except the charity of Fitr for a slave.

    2273–2276The Book of Zakat4
  3. 387

    When Ibn Jamil, Khalid b. Walid and Abbas refused Zakat to Umar, the Prophet faulted Ibn Jamil, defended Khalid's armour kept for Allah, and took Abbas's due upon himself.

    2277The Book of Zakat1
  4. 388

    Zakat-ul-Fitr is one sa' of dates, barley, cheese or raisins for every Muslim, free or slave, paid before the prayer; Abu Sa'id rejected Mu'awiya's smaller measure of wheat.

    2278–2289The Book of Zakat12
  5. 389

    Withholders of dues on gold, silver, camels, cattle or sheep are branded or trampled on Resurrection Day, treasure chasing them as a bald snake; horses bring reward, cover or burden.

    2290–2297The Book of Zakat8
  6. 390

    Bedouins complaining that Zakat collectors wronged them were told to please the collectors, and Jarir said every collector left him pleased after that.

    2298–2299The Book of Zakat2
  7. 391

    The very rich are losers unless generous; the Prophet would keep nothing of an Uhud of gold but for debt; Gabriel said whoever dies associating nothing with Allah enters Paradise.

    2300–2305The Book of Zakat6
  8. 392

    Abu Dharr warned Quraysh hoarders of branding with heated stones, recalled the Prophet's wish to spend an Uhud of gold, and said to take gifts unless they cost one's religion.

    2306–2307The Book of Zakat2
  9. 393

    Allah tells the son of Adam to spend and He will spend on him, for His right hand is full and spending day and night does not diminish it.

    2308–2309The Book of Zakat2
  10. 394

    The best dinar is spent on one's family, withholding food from one's slaves is sin enough, and a man was told to spend on himself first, then family, then relatives.

    2310–2314The Book of Zakat5
  11. 395

    After the verse on giving what you love, Abu Talha gave his garden Bairaha, passed to relatives on the Prophet's advice; charity to kin, even a husband, earns double reward.

    2315–2319The Book of Zakat5
  12. 396

    Umm Salama was told she is rewarded for spending on Abu Salama's sons, and a Muslim's spending on his family, seeking Allah's reward, counts as charity.

    2320–2323The Book of Zakat4
  13. 397

    Asma' asked whether to treat kindly her polytheist mother, who came to her during the treaty with Quraysh, and the Prophet told her to do so.

    2324–2325The Book of Zakat2
  14. 398

    A man whose mother died suddenly without a will was told she would be rewarded if he gave charity on her behalf.

    2326–2327The Book of Zakat2
  15. 399

    Every good act is charity: glorifying Allah, enjoining good, lawful intimacy with a wife, a good word, removing harm from the path, helping the needy, or refraining from evil.

    2328–2335The Book of Zakat8
  16. 400

    Every morning two angels pray, one asking Allah to give more to the one who spends and the other to destroy the one who withholds.

    2336The Book of Zakat1
  17. 401

    A time will come when wealth overflows and none accepts charity, a man is followed by forty women, and the earth casts out gold and silver that is left untouched.

    2337–2341The Book of Zakat5
  18. 402

    Allah accepts charity only from honest earnings, and takes even a date with His right hand, fostering it like a colt until it is larger than a mountain.

    2342–2345The Book of Zakat4
  19. 403

    Allah is good and accepts only good; He told messengers and believers to eat good things, and a traveller's supplication goes unanswered while his food, drink and clothing are unlawful.

    2346The Book of Zakat1
  20. 404

    Shield yourselves from the Fire even with half a date or a good word; Allah will speak to each without interpreter, who sees only his deeds and the Fire ahead.

    2347–2350The Book of Zakat4
  21. 405

    Grieved at the poverty of ragged Mudar tribesmen, the Prophet urged charity till heaps formed, saying one who sets a good or evil precedent shares its followers' reward or burden.

    2351–2354The Book of Zakat4
  22. 406

    When hypocrites mocked a labourer's half sa' gift as unneeded and a larger gift as showing off, a verse was revealed about those who scoff at voluntary givers.

    2355–2356The Book of Zakat2
  23. 407

    Giving a family a she-camel to milk morning and evening earns a great reward, a reward for each morning and evening drink.

    2357–2358The Book of Zakat2
  24. 408

    The Prophet likened the giver and the miser to two men in coats of mail: the giver's expands to cover him wholly, the miser's contracts, each ring gripping its place.

    2359–2361The Book of Zakat3
  25. 409

    Charity given to an adulteress, a rich man and a thief was accepted, as it might restrain her, teach the rich man to spend, and stop the thief stealing.

    2362The Book of Zakat1
  26. 410

    An honest trustee giving as told, a wife giving her husband's food without harm, and a slave feeding the poor from his master's meat all share reward with the owner.

    2363–2370The Book of Zakat8
  27. 411

    Those spending pairs for Allah are called through Paradise's gates, the Prophet hoping Abu Bakr would be called through all; one day Abu Bakr did four deeds that bring Paradise.

    2371–2374The Book of Zakat4
  28. 412

    The Prophet told Asma' to spend and not count or hoard, lest Allah count and withhold from her, and to give according to her means from what Zubair gave her.

    2375–2378The Book of Zakat4
  29. 413

    Allah shades seven on the Day with no other shade, among them a just ruler, a youth raised in worship, one devoted to mosques, and a secret giver of charity.

    2379–2381The Book of Zakat3
  30. 414

    The best charity is given while healthy, close-fisted, fearing poverty and hoping for wealth, not delayed until death, when one says this is for so-and-so though it already is theirs.

    2382–2384The Book of Zakat3
  31. 415

    The giving upper hand is better than the begging lower hand; the best charity leaves the giver self-sufficient and begins with dependents; greedily taken wealth, Hakim was told, is unblessed.

    2385–2388The Book of Zakat4
  32. 416

    The Prophet said he is only a distributor, Allah the Bestower, and what is begged insistently from him is unblessed; the truly poor are needy yet do not beg.

    2389–2395The Book of Zakat7
  33. 417

    Constant beggars meet Allah with fleshless faces; selling firewood is better than begging, allowed only for debt, calamity or attested poverty; some companions pledged never to beg.

    2396–2404The Book of Zakat9
  34. 418

    Umar, and then his collector Ibn al-Sa'di, were told to accept what comes unasked and without greed, even pay for collecting Zakat, but not to long for other wealth.

    2405–2409The Book of Zakat5
  35. 419

    An old heart stays young in loving wealth and long life; given two valleys of riches, man wants a third, only dust fills him, and Allah turns to the repentant.

    2410–2419The Book of Zakat10
  36. 420

    True richness is of the soul; the Prophet feared the world's adornment, likening wealth to spring growth, a help when taken rightly; refraining from begging, contentment and endurance are praised.

    2420–2427The Book of Zakat8
  37. 421

    The Prophet gave even to importunate beggars, saying he was no miser, smiled at a bedouin who pulled his mantle hard and gave him, and kept a cloak for Makhrama.

    2428–2432The Book of Zakat5
  38. 422

    Sa'd pleaded for a man he called a believer; the Prophet said perhaps a Muslim, and that he gives to some over dearer people lest they fall into the Fire.

    2433–2435The Book of Zakat3
  39. 423

    The Prophet gave Hunayn's spoils to Quraysh and new Muslims; the Ansar, who answered his call when others fled, complained; he asked if returning with him did not please them.

    2436–2442The Book of Zakat7
  40. 424

    At Hunayn the Prophet gave chiefs a hundred camels each, completing Abbas b. Mirdas's share, reminded the Ansar of Allah's favours through him, and recalled Moses' patience when called unjust.

    2443–2448The Book of Zakat6
  41. 425

    Sparing men who called his distribution unjust, the Prophet foretold people whose Quran recital never passes their throats, who pass through religion like an arrow, killed by those nearer truth.

    2449–2461The Book of Zakat13
  42. 426

    Ali reported the Prophet's order to kill those whose recital does not pass their throats, fought the Khawarij, and found among their dead the man with a defective hand foretold.

    2462–2472The Book of Zakat11
  43. 427

    Charity is not lawful for the Prophet and his family: he made Hasan drop a charity date, discarded dates that might be charity, and refused to make relatives Zakat collectors.

    2473–2482The Book of Zakat10
  44. 428

    Charity given to Barira, or to Juwayriya's freed maid, became lawful for the Prophet's household: it was charity for her and a gift for them, having reached its destination.

    2483–2489The Book of Zakat7
  45. 429

    Sadaqa mutton sent to Umm Atiyya and passed to Aisha had reached its proper place, and the Prophet ate what came as a gift but not as sadaqa.

    2490–2491The Book of Zakat2
  46. 430

    The Prophet asked Allah to bless those who brought him sadaqa, Abu Aufa's family among them, and the Zakat collector should leave pleased with those he collects from.
