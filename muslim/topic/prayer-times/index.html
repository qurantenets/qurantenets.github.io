    Whoever catches one rak'ah of the dawn prayer before sunrise, or of the afternoon prayer before sunset, has caught that prayer.

  2. 225

    When Umar b. Abd al-Aziz delayed prayer, Urwa reported Gabriel leading the Prophet in the five prayers, and Aisha his afternoon prayer while sunlight was in her room.

    1379–1384The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer6
  3. 226

    The Prophet set the limits of each prayer's time, and showed an inquirer by praying early one day and late the next, saying the time lies between.

    1385–1394The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer10
  4. 227

    The noon prayer is delayed in intense heat till it cools, for such heat is Hell's exhalation; the Fire, complaining, was allowed two breaths, in winter and summer.

    1395–1403The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer9
  5. 228

    The Prophet prayed noon once the sun declined and did not respond to complaints about praying on scorching ground; in the heat some prostrated on their spread cloth.

    1404–1407The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer4
  6. 229

    The afternoon prayer was prayed early enough to reach Quba or cook and eat a camel before sunset; the hypocrite waits till the sun is between the devil's horns.

    1408–1416The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer9
  7. 230

    Whoever misses the afternoon prayer is as though he has been deprived of his family and his property.

    1417–1419The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer3
  8. 235

    The Prophet prayed the evening prayer as soon as the sun had set, and afterwards one could still see where his arrow fell.

    1440–1442The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer3
  9. 236

    The Prophet once delayed the night prayer till many slept, saying no others waited for it and he would pray then were it not a burden on his community.

    1443–1446The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer4
  10. 237

    The Prophet sometimes delayed Isha until people slept or near midnight, said those waiting were in prayer, and would have ordered that hour were it not hard on his Ummah.

    1447–1454The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer8
  11. 238

    The Prophet told people not to let the bedouins prevail over the name of their night prayer, which is Isha in the Book of Allah, though bedouins call it Atama.

    1455–1456The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer2
  12. 239

    Believing women prayed the morning prayer with the Prophet and went home wrapped in their mantles, unrecognisable in the darkness before dawn.

    1457–1459The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer3
  13. 240

    Jabir and Abu Barza described the Prophet's prayer times, from noon in the midday heat to dawn in early darkness; he disliked sleeping before Isha and talking after it.

    1460–1464The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer5
  14. 241

    The Prophet told Abu Dharr that under rulers who delay prayer beyond its time he should pray at its proper time and count praying with them as voluntary.

    1465–1471The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer7
  15. 259

    On journeys the Prophet and his companions slept past sunrise, moved on, then prayed the dawn prayer; he said sleep is no fault, and a little water sufficed everyone.

    1560–1565The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer6
  16. 260

    Whoever forgets a prayer or sleeps through it should pray it when he remembers, its only expiation, for Allah said to observe prayer for His remembrance.

    1566–1569The Book of Mosques and Places of Prayer4
  17. 267

    The Prophet combined noon with afternoon and sunset with Isha in Medina without fear, rain or journey, and on the Tabuk expedition, so his Ummah would not face hardship.

    1628–1637The Book of Prayer - Travellers10
  18. 320

    The Prophet forbade praying after the dawn prayer till sunrise, after Asr till sunset, and as the sun rises, stands at midday or sets, for it rises between Satan's horns.
