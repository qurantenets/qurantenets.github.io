Sahih al-Bukhari · Book 58 · 34 hadith

Jizyah and Mawaada'ah

27 flows · 100% of hadith grouped · hadith Nos. 3156–3189

#TopicHadithCount
  1. 1141

    Umar orders close-kin marriages among Magians annulled, and takes jizya from Magians only after Abdur-Rahman bin Auf testifies the Prophet took it from the Magians of Hajar.

    3156–3157Jizyah and Mawaada'ah2
  2. 1142

    As Abu Ubaida brings Bahrain's jizya, the Prophet tells the Ansar he fears not their poverty but luxury, over which they will compete and be destroyed like past nations.

    3158Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  3. 1143

    Advised by Al-Hurmuzan to strike Khosrau first, Umar sends An-Nu'man; Al-Mughira tells Khosrau's envoy their Prophet ordered them to fight until they worship Allah alone or pay jizya.

    3159Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  4. 1144

    Umar sends Muslims against Khosrau; al-Mughira tells Khosrau's envoy the Prophet ordered them to fight until Allah alone is worshipped or Jizya paid, and an-Nu'man waits till midday.

    3160Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  5. 1145

    At Tabuk the Prophet gives Aila's king, who sent him a mule and cloak, a treaty keeping his authority; Umar urges keeping Allah's covenant with the Dhimmis, the Prophet's convention.

    3161–3162Jizyah and Mawaada'ah2
  6. 1146

    Offered land in Bahrain, the Ansar refuse unless their Quraishi brothers get the same, and the Prophet tells them to be patient with the preference they will see after him.

    3163Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  7. 1147

    Abu Bakr pays Jabir what the Prophet promised him from Bahrain's revenue; the Prophet distributes Bahrain's money in the mosque till none remains, refusing to help al-Abbas lift his share.

    3164–3165Jizyah and Mawaada'ah2
  8. 1148

    Whoever kills a person who has a treaty with the Muslims will not smell the fragrance of Paradise, though it can be perceived from forty years' distance.

    3166Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  9. 1149

    At Bait-ul-Midras the Prophet tells the Jews they will be safe if they embrace Islam, that he wants to expel them, and that they may sell their property.

    3167Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  10. 1150

    Ibn Abbas weeps recalling Thursday, when the gravely ill Prophet asked for a scapula to write on, people disputed, and he ordered pagans expelled from Arabia and delegates given gifts.

    3168Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  11. 1151

    After Khaibar's conquest the Prophet questions the Jews about a poisoned sheep given him; they admit poisoning it to learn whether he was a liar or a prophet.

    3169Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  12. 1152

    Anas says Qunut is before bowing; the Prophet invoked evil on Bani Sulaim for a month, and grieved most for the Qaris whom pagans killed despite a peace pact.

    3170Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  13. 1153

    The Prophet honours the asylum Um Hani gave a man on the day Mecca was conquered, and Ali's paper says any Muslim's asylum binds all and declares Medina a sanctuary.

    3171–3172Jizyah and Mawaada'ah2
  14. 1154

    Abdullah bin Sahl is found murdered in Khaibar, then under treaty; his kin cannot swear to the killer and reject the Jews' oaths, so the Prophet pays the blood money.

    3173Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  15. 1155

    Heraclius summoned Abu Sufyan and a Quraishi trading caravan in Sham during the truce the Prophet had concluded with Abu Sufyan and the Quraish infidels.

    3174Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  16. 1156

    The Prophet was once bewitched, so that he imagined he had done things he had not done.

    3175Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  17. 1157

    At Tabuk the Prophet names six signs before the Hour: his death, Jerusalem's conquest, a plague, overflowing wealth, an affliction in every Arab house, and Byzantine betrayal after a truce.

    3176Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  18. 1158

    On the day of Nahr Abu Bakr has it announced that no pagan may perform Hajj after that year nor anyone circle the Ka'ba naked, throwing back the pagans' covenant.

    3177Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  19. 1159

    Lying, breaking promises, treachery in covenants and abuse in quarrels make a pure hypocrite, and whoever has one of them has a trait of hypocrisy until he gives it up.

    3178Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  20. 1160

    Ali says they wrote only the Quran and a paper from the Prophet: Medina is a sanctuary, even the lowest Muslim's asylum binds all, and its betrayer is cursed.

    3179Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  21. 1161

    Abu Huraira foretells, from the Prophet, a time when Allah and His Apostle's asylum for the Dhimmis is outraged, and Allah makes them dare to refuse the Jizya.

    3180Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  22. 1162

    At Siffin Sahl bin Hunaif recalls Hudaibiya, when Umar protested the terms to the Prophet and Abu Bakr, and after Surat al-Fath the Prophet told him it was a victory.

    3181–3182Jizyah and Mawaada'ah2
  23. 1163

    During the Quraish peace treaty Asma's pagan mother visits her hoping for a reward from her, and the Prophet tells Asma to keep good relations with her.

    3183Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  24. 1164

    Before an Umrah the Meccans allow the Prophet three days with sheathed arms; he erases 'Apostle of Allah' from the treaty himself when Ali refuses, and leaves after three days.

    3184Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  25. 1165

    When Uqba throws camel intestines on the prostrating Prophet, he invokes Allah against the Quraish chiefs he names, later all killed at Badr and thrown into a well.

    3185Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
  26. 1166

    Every betrayer will have a flag on the Day of Resurrection, fixed or shown so that he is recognised and his betrayal made known.

    3186–3188Jizyah and Mawaada'ah3
  27. 1167

    Conquering Mecca, the Prophet says there is no migration now, only Jihad and intention, and Mecca is a sanctuary whose plants and game are protected, except Idhkhir.

    3189Jizyah and Mawaada'ah1
