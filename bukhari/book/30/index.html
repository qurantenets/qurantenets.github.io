Fasting

22 flows · 100% of hadith grouped · hadith Nos. 1891–2007

#TopicHadithCount
  1. 577

    Ramadan's fast is compulsory with the five prayers and zakat; Ashura, fasted by Quraysh before Islam, became optional once Ramadan was prescribed; fasting is a shield that Allah Himself rewards.

    1891–1894Fasting4
  2. 578

    Hudhaifa said prayer, fasting and charity expiate one's trials, but afflictions spreading like sea waves lie behind a closed gate that will be broken, and Umar knew its meaning.

    1895Fasting1
  3. 579

    The fasting enter Paradise by the gate Ar-Raiyan; in Ramadan Paradise opens, Hell closes and devils are chained; fasting follows the crescent, and fasting Ramadan in faith forgives past sins.

    1896–1901Fasting6
  4. 580

    The Prophet was most generous in Ramadan when Gabriel met him; Allah has no need of a liar's fast; fasting is a shield; those unable to marry should fast.

    1902–1905Fasting4
  5. 581

    Fasting starts and ends with the crescent, Sha'ban completed to thirty days if cloudy; a month has twenty-nine or thirty days: the Prophet's month away from his wives lasted twenty-nine.

    1906–1913Fasting8
  6. 582

    None should fast a day or two before Ramadan; verses allowed relations on fasting nights and eating until dawn's white thread, so suhur may last until Ibn Um Maktum's adhan.

    1914–1921Fasting8
  7. 583

    The Prophet forbade others his continuous fasting, urged suhur for its blessing, and on Ashura had it announced that whoever had eaten should eat no more but fast.

    1922–1924Fasting3
  8. 584

    The Prophet sometimes woke in janaba and fasted after bathing, and kissed and embraced his wives while fasting; whoever eats or drinks forgetfully completes his fast, for Allah fed him.

    1925–1933Fasting9
  9. 585

    Uthman washed his hands, mouth, nose, face, arms and feet as the Prophet did, who promised forgiveness of past sins to one praying two undistracted rak'at after such ablution.

    1934Fasting1
  10. 586

    A man who had intercourse with his wife while fasting could not free a slave, fast two months or feed sixty poor, so fed his family dates given for expiation.

    1935–1937Fasting3
  11. 587

    The Prophet was cupped while fasting and while in Ihram, and Anas said cupping a fasting person was disliked only if it weakened him.

    1938–1940Fasting3
  12. 588

    On journeys the Prophet let people fast or not, broke his Ramadan fast going to Mecca, and seeing a faster being shaded, said fasting on a journey is not righteousness.

    1941–1948Fasting8
  13. 589

    Ibn Umar said feeding the poor instead of fasting was cancelled; Aisha made up missed days in Sha'ban; menstruating women do not fast; guardians fast the dead's missed days.

    1949–1953Fasting5
  14. 590

    The fast ends at sunset as night comes from the east, as the Prophet showed on a journey; people stay on the right path while they hasten to break fast.

    1954–1959Fasting6
  15. 591

    The Prophet had the Ansar told to complete the Ashura fast; afterwards they fasted it with their boys, giving those crying for food wool toys until fast-breaking.

    1960Fasting1
  16. 592

    Out of mercy the Prophet forbade Al-Wisal, fasting continuously without breaking at night, saying his Lord feeds him; when people persisted he fasted on with them until the crescent appeared.

    1961–1967Fasting7
  17. 593

    Salman made Abu Darda break his fast, as Lord, soul and family have rights, and the Prophet agreed; the Prophet fasted and left off by turns, most in Sha'ban.

    1968–1973Fasting6
  18. 594

    Abdullah bin Amr, who fasted daily and prayed all night, was told body, wife and guest have rights: fast three days a month, or at most David's alternate days.

    1974–1980Fasting7
  19. 595

    Abu Huraira was advised to fast three days monthly; fasting, the Prophet declined Um Sulaim's food and prayed for Anas; a man who missed month-end fasts should fast two days.

    1981–1983Fasting3
  20. 596

    The Prophet forbade fasting a Friday alone, chose no special fasting days according to Aisha, and drank milk publicly at Arafat when people disputed whether he was fasting.

    1984–1989Fasting6
  21. 597

    Fasting on both Eid days is forbidden, Ibn Umar citing the ban beside the duty to keep vows; on Tashriq days only pilgrims unable to afford a sacrifice may fast.

    1990–1999Fasting10
  22. 598

    Ashura, fasted by Quraysh before Islam and by Jews as Moses did, was ordered by the Prophet, then left optional once Ramadan was prescribed; he favoured no day's fast more.
