Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)

24 flows · 100% of hadith grouped · hadith Nos. 2738–2781

#TopicHadithCount
  1. 938

    Wills are to be written within two nights; the Prophet left little and bequeathed Allah's Book; Aisha denies a will for Ali; Sa'd's bequest is limited to a third.

    2738–2744Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)7
  2. 939

    Sa'd claims Zam'a's slave girl's son for his brother Utba; the Prophet gives him to Abd bin Zam'a, the child belonging to the bed, telling Sauda to screen herself.

    2745Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  3. 940

    A Jew who crushed a girl's head between two stones, named by her nod and then confessing, had his head crushed with stones on the Prophet's order.

    2746Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  4. 941

    Once offspring inherited and parents only by will, until Allah set shares: a male double a female, a sixth for each parent, and fixed shares for wife and husband.

    2747Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  5. 942

    The best charity is given while healthy, greedy and fearing poverty, not delayed to the deathbed, when the property already belongs to the heirs.

    2748Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  6. 943

    The signs of a hypocrite are three: he lies when he speaks, betrays when entrusted, and breaks his promises.

    2749Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  7. 944

    The Prophet tells Hakim bin Hizam that wealth taken without greed is blessed and the giving hand better, so Hakim never asks again, refusing even his booty share.

    2750Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  8. 945

    Everyone is a guardian responsible for his charge: the ruler for his subjects, a man for his family, a lady for her husband's house, a servant for his master's property.

    2751Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  9. 946

    When 'Warn your nearest kinsmen' came, the Prophet called Quraysh's clans, Safiya and Fatima, saying he cannot save them from Allah's punishment; Abu Talha divides a garden among relatives.

    2752–2753Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)2
  10. 947

    Seeing a man driving his sacrificial camel, the Prophet told him repeatedly to ride it, adding 'woe to you' when he protested it was for sacrifice.

    2754–2755Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)2
  11. 948

    Sa'd gives a garden and must fulfil a vow for his dead mother; Ka'b keeps some property, Abu Talha's garden goes to relatives, and heirs give at the division.

    2756–2762Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)7
  12. 949

    Aisha explains verses 4.2-3: a guardian drawn to an orphan girl's beauty and wealth may marry her only with a full dowry like her peers', otherwise he marries other women.

    2763Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  13. 950

    Umar makes his prized palm garden Thamgh an endowment never sold, gifted or bequeathed, its fruits for charity, its administrator eating fairly without growing rich.

    2764Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  14. 951

    Aisha says verse 4.6 came about an orphan's guardian: if he is poor, he may take what is just and reasonable from the orphan's wealth.

    2765Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  15. 952

    Avoid seven destructive sins: associating others with Allah, sorcery, unjust killing, consuming usury, consuming an orphan's wealth, fleeing the battlefield, and accusing chaste believing women.

    2766Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  16. 953

    Ibn Umar never refused guardianship, Ibn Sirin liked guardians to meet on an orphan's good, Tawus cited verse 2.220, and Ata said guardians provide for orphans from their shares.

    2767Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  17. 954

    Abu Talha brought the boy Anas to serve the Prophet at home and on journeys, and the Prophet never asked him why he did or left anything.

    2768Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  18. 955

    Abu Talha gives his beloved garden Bairuha for Allah's sake and, on the Prophet's advice, divides it among relatives; a man gives a garden in charity for his dead mother.

    2769–2770Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)2
  19. 956

    Banu an-Najjar will take their garden's price for the mosque only from Allah, and Umar, advised by the Prophet, makes his Khaibar land an endowment never sold, gifted or bequeathed.

    2771–2774Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)4
  20. 957

    Umar asks whether he may buy back the horse he gave in charity for fighting, and is told not to, for one should not take back charity.

    2775Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  21. 958

    The Prophet's heirs will not inherit a dinar or dirham, for what he leaves beyond his wives' support and his employees' wages is charity.

    2776Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  22. 959

    Umar's endowment lets its administrator eat and feed a friend without storing; besieged, Uthman recalls buying the well of Ruma and equipping Tabuk's army; Banu an-Najjar seek only Allah's price.

    2777–2779Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)3
  23. 960

    Two men swear a dead companion's silver bowl was lost; when it turns up, his relatives swear their testimony truer, and the verse 'When death approached any of you' came.

    2780Wills and Testaments (Wasaayaa)1
  24. 961

    Jabir's father, martyred at Uhud, left heavy debts; the Prophet sat on the biggest date heap and measured out until all were paid, the heaps still seeming whole.
