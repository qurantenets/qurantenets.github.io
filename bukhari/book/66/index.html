  1. 1845

    The Quran came to the Prophet over ten years in Mecca, most heavily before his death, in Quraysh's dialect; Gabriel looked like Dihya, and revelation left the Prophet red-faced.

    After Yamama Abu Bakr has Zaid bin Thabit collect the Quran; Uthman sends a copy to every province and has other copies burnt; Zaid also wrote verses for the Prophet.

    Gabriel recited the Quran to the Prophet in seven ways, and when Umar objects to Hisham's different reading of al-Furqan, the Prophet says both were revealed so.

    Aisha explains why a surah mentioning Paradise and the Fire came before laws, and dictates the surahs in order; companions name surahs learnt early and paired surahs the Prophet recited.

    4993–4996Virtues of the Qur'an4
  5. 1849

    Gabriel met the Prophet every night of Ramadan to go over the Quran, once a year and twice in the year he died, when he stayed twenty days in Itikaf.

    4997–4998Virtues of the Qur'an2

    The Prophet names four companions to learn the Quran from; Ibn Mas'ud knows where every surah came down, four Ansar collected it, and Ubai was the best reciter.

  7. 1851

    Al-Fatiha is the most superior surah and cured a chief stung by a scorpion, and reciting the last two verses of al-Baqarah at night is sufficient.

    5006–5009Virtues of the Qur'an4
  8. 1852

    A thief caught stealing Zakat food tells Abu Huraira that reciting Ayat al-Kursi at bedtime brings Allah's guard against Satan, and the Prophet says the liar, Satan, spoke the truth.

    Tranquillity and angels descended on reciters of al-Kahf and al-Baqarah; the Prophet loved al-Fath; al-Ikhlas equals a third of the Quran; he recited the refuge surahs and blew over himself.

    5011–5018Virtues of the Qur'an8

    Ibn Abbas and Muhammad bin al-Hanafiyya say the Prophet left nothing except what is between the two bindings of the Quran.

    5019Virtues of the Qur'an1
  11. 1855

    A believer reciting the Quran is like a citron and a wicked reciter like basil; Muslims working from Asr to sunset earn twice the wage of Jews and Christians.

    5020–5021Virtues of the Qur'an2

    Asked if the Prophet made a will, Abdullah bin Abi Aufa first says no, then that he made a will recommending Allah's Book.

    Allah listens to no prophet as He listens to one who recites the Quran aloud in a pleasant tone.

    One may wish to be like only two men: one given the Quran who recites it night and day, and one given wealth who spends it rightly.

    The best of the Muslims are those who learn the Quran and teach it.

    A woman offers herself to the Prophet, who marries her to a man owning not even an iron ring for the amount of the Quran he knows by heart.

    5029–5030Virtues of the Qur'an2
  17. 1861

    Keep reciting the Quran, which escapes faster than tied camels, and do not say 'I forgot'; the Prophet recited al-Fath riding, and Ibn Abbas learnt the Mufassal as a boy.

    5031–5036Virtues of the Qur'an7
  18. 1862

    A reciter reminds the Prophet of missed verses; saying 'I forgot' is disliked, the Quran was revealed in several readings, and reciting the Mufassal in a day is too quick.

    5037–5043Virtues of the Qur'an8
  19. 1863

    The Prophet moved his tongue hastily as Gabriel brought revelation, so Allah revealed that He would collect it in his mind; afterwards he listened, then recited it.

    5044Virtues of the Qur'an1
  20. 1864

    The Prophet prolonged sounds when reciting and recited al-Fath softly in a vibrating tone on his camel; he tells Abu Musa he has a melodious voice like David's family.

  21. 1865

    The Prophet likes hearing the Quran from others and weeps as Ibn Mas'ud recites an-Nisa; Ibn Shubruma holds three verses the least to recite, and al-Baqarah's last two suffice.

    5049–5051.2Virtues of the Qur'an4
  22. 1866

    Abdullah bin Amr, fasting daily and finishing the Quran nightly, is told to finish it in a month, then no less than seven days; the Prophet weeps hearing Ibn Mas'ud.

    The Prophet foretells people who recite the Quran without it passing their throats and who go out of Islam like an arrow through game; Ali's report says to kill them.

    5057–5058Virtues of the Qur'an2
  24. 1868

    A believer who recites the Quran and acts on it is like a citron, and a hypocrite who recites it is like basil, sweet-smelling but bitter.
